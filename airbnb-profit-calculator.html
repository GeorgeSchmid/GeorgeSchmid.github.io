How this calculator works

Net profit = (nightly rate × nights + cleaning fee × stays) − platform fee − (cleaning + supplies) × stays − fixed costs

The break-even figure shows how many booked nights you need to cover your fixed costs, assuming your stays keep the same average length. It's the most useful number for quiet months: if you're below it, lowering your price to win more nights usually costs less than an empty calendar.

Example

An apartment rents for €80 a night and is booked for 20 nights across 6 stays in a 30-day month. Guests pay a €40 cleaning fee, the cleaner costs €30, laundry and supplies €8 per stay, the platform takes 15.5%, and fixed costs are €900.

Gross revenue (20 × €80 + 6 × €40)€1,840.00

That's a 23% margin at 67% occupancy. Break-even is 14 booked nights: below that, this month would lose money.

Frequently asked questions

How do I calculate Airbnb profit?

Add up everything guests paid you (nightly rate × nights plus cleaning fees), subtract the platform fee, subtract the costs of each stay (cleaning, laundry, supplies), and then subtract your fixed monthly costs such as rent or mortgage, utilities, internet and insurance. What's left is your profit before tax.

What profit margin is normal for a short-term rental?

It varies a lot by location, season and whether you own or rent the property. The useful check is your own margin month by month: off-season months often fall below zero once fixed costs are included, even when the yearly total is positive.

Should I include my mortgage in the calculation?

For cash flow, yes: include the full monthly payment so you know what's left in your pocket. For tax purposes, rules differ by country (often only the interest part is deductible), so ask a local accountant.

What is break-even occupancy?

It's the number of booked nights you need each month to cover all your costs. Below it you lose money; every night above it adds profit. This calculator shows it in nights and as a percentage of the month.
